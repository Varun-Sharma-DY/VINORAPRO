# DIRECTION.md — Vinora Website

## 1. Design Objective

Create a professional, visually refined website that communicates Vinora's technical competence in custom ERP development and business automation.

The website should make potential clients feel that Vinora is capable of understanding complex business operations and translating them into well-designed software.

The objective is not to create the flashiest website.

The objective is to create a website that looks considered, sophisticated, credible and technically capable.

## 2. Brand Identity

- Existing company logo must be retained.
- Existing brand identity is primarily blue and white.
- Exact brand colour codes are yet to be established.
- The design should complement the existing logo.
- Do not invent a completely different brand identity without approval.

## 3. Desired Visual Characteristics

The website should feel:

- Premium but restrained.
- Modern and technically sophisticated.
- Minimal without feeling empty.
- Structured and visually balanced.
- Professional without feeling like a traditional corporate website.
- Confident without being visually aggressive.

## 4. Design Principles

### Visual Hierarchy
Every page should have a clear visual hierarchy.

Headings, supporting copy, imagery and CTAs should guide the visitor naturally.

### Typography
Typography should be distinctive, readable and consistent.

Avoid excessively decorative fonts and generic typography combinations.

### Layout
Use deliberate spacing, strong composition and varied section layouts.

Avoid repeating the same card layout throughout the website.

### Colour
Use the existing blue and white brand direction as the starting point.

Additional neutral or complementary colours may be explored, subject to approval.

### Visual Assets
Prefer relevant software interfaces, custom diagrams, workflow visualisations and purposeful graphics over generic stock photographs.

Actual company screenshots and portfolio material will be supplied during the final phase.

### Animation
Motion should be subtle, intentional and responsive.

Animations should improve the perceived quality of interactions rather than distract visitors.

Avoid excessive parallax, bouncing elements and unnecessary entrance animations.

## 5. Things to Avoid

- Generic AI-generated website layouts.
- Predictable SaaS landing-page templates.
- Excessive gradient backgrounds.
- Unnecessary glassmorphism.
- Oversized empty hero sections.
- Repetitive three-card service sections.
- Excessive rounded containers.
- Decorative animations without purpose.
- Generic stock photographs of office teams.
- Excessive text and unnecessary visual elements.
- Flashy effects that compromise performance.

## 6. Reference-Based Design

No external website references have been approved yet.

Claude must independently propose three distinct design directions using the Taste Skill.

Each direction must include:

- Design concept and name.
- Visual mood.
- Typography pairing.
- Colour palette with HEX codes.
- Layout philosophy.
- Motion intensity.
- Visual density.
- Design variance.

The directions must be genuinely different, not minor variations of the same template.

## 7. Design Approval Rules

Do not begin full website development before the design direction is reviewed and approved.

Save the final approved decisions in this file.

The final approved direction must document:

- Selected design concept.
- Typography.
- Primary and secondary colours.
- Background and surface colours.
- Layout rules.
- Spacing principles.
- Component styling.
- Motion principles.
- Taste Skill settings.

## 8. Design Quality Standard

The final website should look like it was deliberately designed by a capable digital agency.

It should not look like an automatically generated template.

Visual quality, usability, consistency and performance must be treated as equally important.
